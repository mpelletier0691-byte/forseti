# Surviving (or Bringing) Summary Judgment

**Rule 56** lets either side ask the court to decide some or all of the case **without a trial** because the material facts are not genuinely disputed.

## The standard

> Summary judgment is granted when the movant shows there is **no genuine dispute as to any material fact** and the movant is **entitled to judgment as a matter of law**.

A "material" fact is one that affects the outcome under the governing law. A "genuine" dispute exists when a reasonable jury could find for the nonmoving party on that fact.

## The mechanics

1. **Statement of undisputed material facts** - numbered, each fact cited to a piece of admissible evidence (declaration, deposition page/line, exhibit).
2. **Memorandum of law** - apply the law to the undisputed facts.
3. **Affidavits / declarations** - sworn statements made on personal knowledge.
4. **Exhibits** - authenticated documents.

## If you are opposing

- Pay surgical attention to the moving party's numbered fact statement. **Admit, deny, or qualify** each one with a record citation. Many courts deem unanswered facts **admitted**.
- A "scintilla" of evidence is not enough. Show evidence a reasonable juror could believe.
- Authenticate every document you attach.
- Object to inadmissible evidence (hearsay, lack of foundation).

## If you are moving

- File only if you have **clean** evidence. A losing motion educates the other side and strengthens their trial prep.
- Move on **specific** issues if not all (Rule 56(a) lets you move on a claim or defense or a part).
- Address every element of every claim you want disposed of.

## Timing

- Rule 56(b): "any time until 30 days after the close of all discovery" - unless the scheduling order says otherwise.
- Local rules often impose page limits. Check before you write.

## Common pitfalls

- Briefing only **liability** when **damages** also have triable issues.
- Citing your **complaint** as evidence (it isn't).
- Forgetting **affidavits must be made on personal knowledge** (Rule 56(c)(4)).
- Filing inadmissible **hearsay** without showing the underlying source could be admissible at trial.
