# Responding to a Motion

Most pro se losses are not because the law was bad. They are because someone missed a **deadline** or never filed an **opposition** at all. When a motion lands on your doorstep, do these four things in order:

## 1. Read it. Twice.

Note three things on a sticky note:

1. The **rule** the motion cites (e.g., FRCP 12(b)(6), 56, 60(b)).
2. The **relief** the moving party wants.
3. The **date it was filed** (look at the clerk's stamp or ECF docket entry).

## 2. Compute your deadline immediately

Most federal courts give you **14 days** to oppose under their local rules. Some give 21. Compute the deadline using **Rule 6**:

- Day 0 is the day the motion was served on you.
- Count weekdays AND weekends.
- If the deadline lands on a weekend or federal holiday, it rolls to the next business day.

Forseti's **Deadline Tracker** can compute this for you.

## 3. Write your response

A federal opposition typically has:

- **Caption** identical to the motion.
- **Title** like "Opposition to Defendant's Motion to Dismiss".
- **Introduction** (1 paragraph): what the motion says, why it should be denied.
- **Statement of facts** (only the facts that matter to the motion).
- **Argument** with **headings**, addressing each ground of the motion separately.
- **Conclusion** asking the court to **deny** the motion.
- **Signature block** and **Certificate of Service**.

If the motion is a **motion to dismiss under Rule 12(b)(6)**, your opposition has to show that the complaint contains enough facts to make the claim **plausible** - not just possible (Twombly / Iqbal).

## 4. File and serve before the deadline

- File with the clerk (paper or ECF).
- Serve every other party by the means listed in **Rule 5(b)** - email is fine if everyone consented in writing.
- Keep a stamped copy.

## Tips that save cases

- **Never miss the deadline waiting for documents.** File on time and supplement later if you must.
- **Cite the rule.** Even if you are wrong on a point, citing the right rule shows the judge you took it seriously.
- **Match the moving party's structure.** If they have five arguments, write five sections.
- **End with the exact relief.** "Defendant requests that the Court DENY the Motion to Dismiss in its entirety."

> Information only. Not legal advice. Local rules vary by district.
