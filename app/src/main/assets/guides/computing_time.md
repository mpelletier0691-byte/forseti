# Computing Time Under Rule 6

Civil deadlines are unforgiving. **Rule 6(a)** is the federal counting cheat sheet.

## The basic rule (periods stated in days)

1. **Exclude** the day of the event that triggers the period.
2. **Count every day**, including weekends and federal legal holidays.
3. **Include** the last day, **unless** the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday - in that case, the period continues to the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday.

> Example: a motion is served on a Friday and the rule gives you 14 days to oppose. Day 1 is Saturday. Day 14 is the second Friday after service. If that day is a holiday, the deadline rolls to the next business day.

## Periods stated in hours

Same idea, but you count from the moment of the event and the period continues to the next non-weekend / non-holiday day at the same hour.

## Service by mail / electronic means (Rule 6(d))

When a paper is served by mail or by electronic means and the recipient has a period of time to respond, **add 3 days** to that period - except when the deadline runs from a notice of an order or judgment (those don't get the extra 3).

## Federal legal holidays (Rule 6(a)(6))

- New Year's Day
- Martin Luther King Jr. Day
- Washington's Birthday
- Memorial Day
- Juneteenth
- Independence Day
- Labor Day
- Columbus Day
- Veterans Day
- Thanksgiving Day
- Christmas Day

Plus any day declared a holiday by the President or Congress, and (for state law deadlines) holidays declared by the state where the court sits.

## What "filing" means

A paper is filed when it is delivered to the **clerk** during business hours - **not** when it leaves your printer. Plan accordingly. Most courts have a drop box for after-hours pro se filings; check yours.

## Quick references

- Answer to complaint after personal service: **21 days** (Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(i))
- Answer if waiver of service signed: **60 days** (Rule 12(a)(1)(A)(ii))
- Reply to counterclaim: **21 days** (Rule 12(a)(1)(B))
- Local opposition to motion: usually **14 or 21 days** (check your district's local rules)
- Rule 59 / 60(b)(1)-(3): **28 days** / **1 year** maximums
