# Drafts Tab: Federal Rules, Local Rules, and State Court

Forseti’s **Drafts** tab gives you printable PDFs: official **federal** court forms (where we ship the PDF) and **generated** shells for common filings (motions, discovery, certificates of service, and similar).

## What the drafts are based on

- **Generated** drafts (motions, answers, discovery sets, Rule 26(f) report, etc.) are structured around the **Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (FRCP)**. Each template names the rule it is meant to help you satisfy (for example FRCP 12(b)(6), 33, 56). They are **not** tailored to any one **state’s** rules of civil procedure.
- **Bundled** PDFs in Drafts are **federal** forms from USCourts.gov (for example Pro Se complaints, in forma pauperis). Citations on those forms point to **federal** statutes and rules (for example FRCP 8, 28 U.S.C. § 1332, 42 U.S.C. § 1983), not a state code.

So: **think “federal district court practice,” not “my state’s civil practice act,”** when you pick a draft from this tab.

## What you must still check yourself

1. **Which court you are in.** If your case is in **state** court, your state’s rules of civil procedure, pleading format, and motion practice **replace** FRCP for most purposes. A federal-style motion shell may be the wrong shape or cite the wrong rules for that court.
2. **Local rules** of the district or division (federal) or county (state). Even in federal court, **local rules** and **individual judges’ standing orders** can require extra sections, page limits, font sizes, or a separate memorandum. The footers on generated drafts remind you to verify local requirements before filing.
3. **Substance.** The templates are **organizers and blanks**, not legal advice. You supply the facts, legal arguments, and citations your case needs.

## How Forseti still helps in state court

Use the **State Rules** tab for your jurisdiction, **Uploaded Rules** for local rules and orders, **Quick Jump** for FRCP when federal rules also apply (for example some hybrid questions), and **Case Profiles** + deadlines to stay on calendar. The Drafts tab remains useful when you are in **federal** court or when you intentionally adapt a federal shell with local counsel or self-help review.

---

**Bottom line:** Drafts in Forseti are built around **federal** civil procedure and federal forms—not state-specific rules. Always confirm **court, state, local, and judge** requirements before filing anything.
