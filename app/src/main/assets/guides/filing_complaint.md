# Filing a Civil Complaint

A complaint is the document that **starts** a federal civil lawsuit. Get it wrong and the court will dismiss it before the other side ever responds.

## What a complaint must contain (Rule 8)

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a) requires three things, in this order:

1. **Jurisdictional statement.** A short statement of the grounds for the court's jurisdiction (federal question, diversity, supplemental, etc.).
2. **Statement of the claim.** A short and plain statement showing you are entitled to relief.
3. **Demand for relief.** What you want the court to do (money, injunction, declaration).

> Plain English: tell the court *why it can hear this*, *what happened*, and *what you want*.

## Form & layout (Rule 10)

- A **caption** with the court name, parties, case number (left blank until the clerk assigns one), and the title "COMPLAINT".
- **Numbered paragraphs**, one fact per paragraph as far as possible.
- Counts (causes of action) labeled clearly: "Count I - Negligence", etc.

## Pro Se forms you can use

The federal courts publish official fill-in complaint forms. They are **public domain** and listed in the **Motion Drafts** tab:

- *Pro Se 1* - general civil complaint
- *Pro Se 2* - diversity jurisdiction complaint
- *Pro Se 7* - employment discrimination
- *Pro Se 14* - civil rights (42 U.S.C. Sec. 1983)
- *Pro Se 15* - federal officer (Bivens)

## Filing checklist

- [ ] Original complaint, signed under Rule 11.
- [ ] Filing fee (current federal fee is $405) **or** an **AO 240** in forma pauperis application.
- [ ] **Civil Cover Sheet (JS-44)** - your district probably requires it.
- [ ] **Summons (AO 440)** for each defendant.
- [ ] **Disclosure statement (Rule 7.1)** if you are a corporation or in a diversity case.
- [ ] Two copies for your records.

## Where to file

- The **Clerk of Court** for the judicial district that has venue (Rule 4(b), 28 U.S.C. Sec. 1391).
- Most districts now require **electronic filing (CM/ECF)**. Pro se litigants can usually file in person or by mail; ask the clerk.

## After filing

1. The clerk issues your **summons**.
2. You have **90 days** to serve each defendant (Rule 4(m)).
3. Defendant has **21 days** after service to answer (Rule 12(a)) - unless they waive service, then 60 days.

## What to avoid

- **Bare conclusions.** "Defendant was negligent" is not enough. Plead facts.
- **Lumping defendants.** Each defendant should know what *they* did.
- **Asking for relief outside the court's power** (e.g., criminal punishment in a civil case).
- **Skipping the verification** when a statute or local rule requires it.

> Forseti is information, not legal advice. Filing a complaint that fails Rule 8 can result in dismissal **with prejudice**. When the stakes are real, talk to a licensed attorney in your jurisdiction.
