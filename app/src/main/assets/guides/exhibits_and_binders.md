# Exhibits and Trial Binders

Exhibits win cases. Disorganized exhibits lose them. The work of building a
clean binder starts long before trial.

## Numbering and labeling

- Number plaintiff exhibits 1, 2, 3 and defendant exhibits 101, 102, 103 (or
  whatever convention your judge uses — *check the standing order*).
- Label every page in the bottom-right corner with the exhibit number.
- Keep a master *exhibit list*: number, description, sponsoring witness, and
  whether it has been admitted.

## The binder

A standard trial binder has:

1. Tab dividers for each exhibit.
2. The exhibit list at the front.
3. Witness outlines behind each exhibit they will introduce.
4. A copy for the judge, a copy for the witness, a copy for opposing counsel,
   and your working copy.

## Foundation, briefly

Before an exhibit comes in, you must establish:

- Authentication (Federal Rule of Evidence 901 — *who made it, when, how*).
- Relevance (FRE 401–403).
- An applicable hearsay exception if the exhibit is a statement (FRE 801–807).
- The original-document rule when applicable (FRE 1001–1008).

## Pretrial duties

Most pretrial orders require you to *exchange exhibit lists* and resolve
objections in advance. Do not skip this step — courts often deem objections
waived if not raised in the pretrial order.
