# Discovery Requests Checklist

Discovery is information exchange. It happens after the pleadings settle in,
typically after the Rule 26(f) conference, and it has its own paperwork rituals.
This checklist gets you organized, not exhaustive.

## Before sending anything

- Confirm the *initial disclosures* under Rule 26(a)(1) have been exchanged.
- Confirm the case has a *scheduling order* (Rule 16) so you know the discovery
  cut-off and the limits the judge has set on numbers of requests.
- Identify your *theory of the case* in two or three sentences. Every request
  should support that theory.

## Interrogatories (Rule 33)

- Default cap: 25 questions, including discrete subparts.
- Number them sequentially and group related items.
- The other side has 30 days to answer (Rule 33(b)(2)).

## Requests for Production (Rule 34)

- Ask for documents *and* electronically stored information (ESI).
- Specify a reasonable form of production (PDF, native, etc.).
- 30-day response window.

## Requests for Admission (Rule 36)

- Use these to lock in undisputed facts so you do not have to prove them at
  trial.
- A request can be deemed admitted if not answered within 30 days.

## Common pitfalls

- *Compound questions* invite objections. Keep each request to one fact.
- *Overbroad time frames* invite proportionality objections. Anchor each
  request to dates relevant to the claims.
- *Failing to confer* before bringing a motion to compel will get the motion
  bounced. Document your meet-and-confer attempts.
