# Service of Process Without Mistakes

If service is bad, the court has no power over the defendant - and the case can be dismissed even years later. Get this right.

## What "service" means

You must deliver a copy of the **summons** and **complaint** to each defendant in a way that **Rule 4** allows. You also have to **prove** you did it.

## Who can serve

- Anyone who is **at least 18** and **not a party** to the case (Rule 4(c)(2)).
- Most pro se litigants ask the **U.S. Marshals** (when authorized in IFP cases) or hire a private process server.

## How to serve - individuals (Rule 4(e))

You can use any method allowed by:

- the law of the **state** where the district court sits, OR
- the law of the **state** where service is made,

OR you can:

1. Personally hand the papers to the defendant, OR
2. Leave them at the defendant's **home** with someone of suitable age living there, OR
3. Deliver them to an **agent** authorized to receive process.

## How to serve - corporations (Rule 4(h))

Deliver to an **officer**, a **managing or general agent**, or any **agent authorized by appointment or by law** to receive process. State Secretary of State websites publish each company's registered agent.

## How to serve - the United States (Rule 4(i))

Specific belt-and-suspenders rules:

1. Serve the **U.S. Attorney** for the district (or a designated assistant), AND
2. Send a copy by **registered or certified mail** to the **Attorney General**, AND
3. If you are challenging an officer or agency, also serve that **officer or agency** by registered/certified mail.

## Waiver of service (Rule 4(d))

The cleanest path. Mail the defendant the complaint plus a **Notice and Request to Waive Service**. If they sign and return it within 30 days, you save service costs and the defendant gets **60 days** (instead of 21) to answer.

## The 90-day clock (Rule 4(m))

You have **90 days** from the day you filed the complaint to serve every defendant. Miss it and the court must either dismiss without prejudice or order you to serve within a specified time.

## Proof of service (Rule 4(l))

File a **return of service** showing exactly how, when, where, and on whom service was made. The process server signs it. Without proof, the court treats the defendant as not served.

## Things that void service

- Serving the **wrong** person at the right address.
- Serving an **employee** of a corporation who is not an officer or agent.
- Mailing without a signed return receipt where one is required.
- Letting the **90 days** pass.
