# Your First Hearing: What to Expect

## The day before

- **Re-read** every paper that was filed on the issue being argued.
- **Outline** your three strongest points - one sentence each.
- **Print** copies of any exhibit you might want to hand to the judge.
- **Plan** your route to the courthouse with a 30-minute buffer.

## What to bring

- Government-issued photo ID.
- A pen and a notepad. No phones in many courtrooms.
- A folder for each motion that contains: the motion, your opposition, and any cited cases.
- Two copies of anything you may try to admit.

## What to wear

Conservative business clothing. Nothing with logos. Cover tattoos if practical. Judges notice.

## Courtroom etiquette

- Arrive at least 20 minutes early. Sit in the gallery until your case is called.
- Stand when the judge enters and exits.
- Always address the court as **"Your Honor."**
- Do not interrupt - not even when the other side misstates a fact. Note it and respond when it is your turn.
- Do not argue with opposing counsel directly. Speak to the judge.
- When you sit down, write down any question the judge asked so you can address it.

## How a motion hearing usually flows

1. The judge calls the case.
2. The **moving party** speaks first (usually 5-10 minutes).
3. The **opposing party** responds.
4. The moving party gets a brief reply.
5. The judge asks questions or rules from the bench, or "takes it under advisement" and rules later in writing.

## Speak in this order

1. **Restate your relief.** "Your Honor, I respectfully request that the Court deny Defendant's Motion to Dismiss."
2. **One-sentence theme.** "This case turns on whether the Complaint pleads facts plausibly showing intent."
3. **Two or three best points** in numbered order.
4. **Direct response** to anything the judge has asked.
5. **Close.** "For these reasons I ask the Court to deny the motion."

## After the hearing

- Order the **transcript** if anything important happened orally.
- File anything you promised to file by the deadline the judge set.
